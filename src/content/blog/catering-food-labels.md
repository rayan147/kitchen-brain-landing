Six deli containers in the walk-in, all the same pale yellow. One is aioli, one is the lemon tahini dressing, and one's been there since last Friday. They all say "sauce" in marker, if they say anything.

Tomorrow someone who didn't make them has to pick one up and put it on food for 120 people.

A label is the note you leave the next cook, from the person who made it.

## Two labels, two jobs

Before deciding what goes on a label, decide who reads it.

- **The kitchen label** goes on the container. It's for your crew: what this is, when it was made, when it has to go, what's in it that could hurt someone.
- **The guest-facing label** goes on food you hand over or sell packaged: a grab-and-go box, a jar of dressing. It's for the person eating it, and the rules for it are stricter.

Most catering kitchens need the first every day and the second some of the time.

## What goes on the kitchen label

At minimum:

1. **What it is.** "Lemon tahini dressing", not "sauce".
2. **How much and how it's held.** "4 qt · Refrigerated", or "Frozen".
3. **When it was made**, or frozen, thawed or opened.
4. **When it has to go.** A use-by date, or "Keep frozen".
5. **What it contains.** The allergens, in capitals, where nobody can miss them.
6. **What it's for.** The event and the date, if it's for one.
7. **Who made it.** Initials, so there's someone to ask.

## Count the use-by date the way the Food Code does

The [FDA Food Code](https://www.fda.gov/food/retail-food-protection/fda-food-code) gives ready-to-eat food that needs to stay cold, held at 41°F or below, up to 7 days, counting the day it was made as day 1. Your state or county may set its own rule, so check with them.

```text
Made: Tuesday, October 13
Shelf life: 7 days, made day = day 1
Use by = Oct 13 + 7 − 1 days
       = Monday, October 19
```

The common mistake is counting from the day after. That's an eighth day nobody planned for.

Freezing stops the clock, so a frozen label says **KEEP FROZEN**, not a date. Thawing starts it again, but only you know how many days it used up before it went in the freezer. A thawed label should say when it thawed, and you decide the rest.

## The allergen line is the one that matters

"CONTAINS: SESAME" on the dressing is the line that keeps someone out of the hospital. It has to come from the recipe, not from memory, because the cook labelling at 6 a.m. didn't make the dressing.

And a blank line has to mean something. If nobody checked an ingredient's allergens, a handwritten label with nothing on it looks exactly like one for food that has none.

## How CostCook prints them

Kitchen labels print from the order's **Prep** and **Pack** pages, or from a recipe or ingredient. The label carries the name, the amount and how it's held, **KEEP FROZEN** or **USE BY** with the date, the made or thawed date, **CONTAINS** and **May contain**, the event and its date, your business name, the initials of who made it, and "2 of 6" when one batch fills several containers.

The allergens come from your recipes and their sub-recipes. If an ingredient's allergens were never reviewed, the label still prints, and says how many: "2 ingredients not reviewed". The Pack page warns you before you print. A blank allergen line never hides an unreviewed ingredient.

Use-by comes from the shelf life you set on the recipe, counted with the day it was made as day 1. You can override it for one print, or print the made date only. With no shelf life set, it prints no use-by rather than guessing one.

It prints through your browser's print dialog, onto a 30-up letter sheet (Avery-compatible), a 58 mm or 62 mm continuous roll, or a 2 × 1 in die-cut roll.

## The guest-facing label is a different job

If you sell packaged food, federal and state rules ask for more. The major allergens have to be declared. On top of that come an ingredient list in order of weight, a Nutrition Facts panel, the net amount, your name and address. Which of these apply to you depends on what you sell and where, so ask your health department.

CostCook's recipe **Nutrition label** gives you a calculated Nutrition Facts panel, the ingredient list and the allergens. Read what it says about itself: the nutrition is a calculated estimate, the ingredients are in recipe order, not by weight, and it isn't a claim of retail-label compliance. Use it to answer a client's question, not as a finished retail label.

## In the walk-in

- Every container says what it is, not "sauce".
- The use-by date counts the day it was made as day 1.
- The allergen line came from the recipe, and anything not reviewed says so.
- Anything frozen says **KEEP FROZEN**, and anything thawed says when.

Tomorrow the cook reaches for the yellow one that says LEMON TAHINI DRESSING, USE BY OCT 19, CONTAINS: SESAME, and doesn't have to ask anybody.
