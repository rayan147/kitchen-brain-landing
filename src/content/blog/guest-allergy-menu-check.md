The planner forwards one line from the bride's sister: "My husband can't have sesame. Is that OK?" You run the menu in your head. Roast chicken, no. Green beans, no. Hummus, yes, obviously, it's tahini. So you swap the hummus and write back "We've got him."

Then the green beans go out with the dressing you always make. The one with a spoon of tahini in it.

Reading by dish name catches the hummus and misses the dressing.

## Put the guest on the order

What you need to know is whether this guest can eat each dish, so the guest goes on the order, next to the food.

In CostCook, open the order and find **Guests' restrictions**. Press **Add a restriction**, pick **Allergen**, then **Sesame**. **Who (optional)** takes a note like "table 4" or "bride's brother-in-law". **How many guests** is one.

From then on every dish on the order is checked against sesame. If nobody declared anything, mark that too: on paper, an empty list looks the same as a list nobody asked about.

## Check down to the last spoonful

Sesame rarely sits in the dish name. It's in a dressing, a spice mix, a bun, a sub-recipe you made in July and forgot. Work it like this:

1. **Every ingredient has an answer** for each of the nine major allergens: **Contains**, **May contain**, **Cross-contact risk**, **Confirmed free from** or **Not sure**.
2. **Sub-recipes count.** The dressing is part of the green beans. If the dressing has tahini, so do the beans.
3. **A dish is only as known as its least-known ingredient.** One ingredient nobody checked makes the whole dish unknown.

CostCook rolls this up through the sub-recipes, and the recipe's allergen chart shows where it came from: sesame **(from Lemon tahini dressing)**. The nine are the ones US labels have to declare: milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soy and sesame. The [FDA's food allergy page](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies) lists the same nine.

## Read the three answers honestly

For that guest, each dish lands in one of three places:

- **A conflict.** An ingredient is confirmed to contain sesame. The order says so in a line like "Conflict · Green beans: tahini (contains)", followed by who it's for. Take it off his plate or change the dish.
- **A check.** Something may contain sesame, there's a cross-contact risk, or an ingredient was never answered. CostCook can't say yes or no, so it says check, and names the ingredient or tells you how many weren't reviewed.
- **Nothing to flag.** Every ingredient was reviewed and none of them contains sesame. When the whole order gets there, it reads "No conflicts with the guests' restrictions".

Unknown is never clear. A blank in your notes means nobody asked yet.

## Answer the blanks before the order goes out

Start from the label on the package. A name only gets you so far: it can tell you butter has milk, not that the rolls from the new bakery have no sesame on the bottom.

On the ingredient, **Draft with Sage** reads the name and suggests answers. It only ever suggests contains or may contain, never free from, and nothing is saved until you take it or answer yourself. Free from is your call, made with the package in your hand.

If you skip an ingredient, it stays skipped on purpose: every order that uses it says check for its allergens until someone answers.

## What happens when you confirm

Before **Confirm order**, the checklist shows **Review allergens** with how many dishes still have unreviewed ingredients. Confirm stays off until they're reviewed. **Confirm anyway** is the owner's alone, and it skips every open check on the order, not just this one. Use it knowing that.

A real conflict doesn't stop you. The confirm dialog lists it under **Guest restriction conflicts to review**, and confirming records that you saw it. That's deliberate: maybe his plate is getting a different side and you've already planned it. The order then shows **Checked at confirm** with the date, and the prep sheet carries the same checks to the cook.

What CostCook doesn't do: it doesn't see your cutting boards, and it can't promise a guest anything. CostCook detects, it never certifies. The promise to the guest is still yours.

## Before you answer the planner

- He's on the order: **Allergen**, **Sesame**, one guest, with a note who.
- Every dish is a conflict, a check or nothing to flag for him, and you know which ingredient decided it.
- Every check was looked at with the package or the supplier, not guessed.
- The conflicts are off his plate, and the cook knows which plate that is.

Then write back "We've got him." This time you'll know which dressing you checked.
