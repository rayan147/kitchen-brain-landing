By the end of this guide, one recipe has its method written in steps, a published version your kitchen cooks from, and a printed sheet that says which version it is and when it went live.

CostCook keeps two things apart: the recipe the kitchen cooks from, and the changes you're still making. You edit a working copy. The kitchen keeps cooking the published version until you publish the next one. So the sheet on the wall always matches a version, and you can see which.

## Before you start

- The recipe is in CostCook with its lines. If it isn't, start with [How to import your ingredients, invoices and recipes](/blog/import-invoices-recipes-ingredient-lists).
- Have the method in your head or on paper: what the cook does, in order.

## Part 1: Write the method and publish it

1. Open **Kitchen records**, then **Recipes**, and open the dish. If it has no method yet, CostCook says so. Press **Go to the method**.

![A notice on the recipe: There is no written method. A cook who has not made this before has only the line list to go on. Go to the method is ringed.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s01-no-method-mobile.png "No method: the cook only has the line list.")

2. Press **+ Add a step**. CostCook asks before it changes a saved recipe. Press **Start working copy**.

![Start a working copy of the saved recipe? A working copy lets you change this recipe privately. The kitchen keeps cooking the saved recipe until you publish the changes. Publish version 1 when it is ready. Discard working copy to keep the saved recipe as it is. Confirmed orders always keep the version they were confirmed with. Start working copy is ringed.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s02-working-copy-mobile.png "The kitchen keeps cooking the saved recipe until you publish.")

3. Fill in one step. Write **What the cook does** in plain words. Add **Hands-on minutes**, **Walk-away minutes** and a **Temperature** with its scale if the step has them. Under **Which lines this step uses**, tick the ingredients for this step. Then press **Add step**.

![The step form: What the cook does: Pour in the stock, cover, and braise until a fork slides in with no push. Part of the job Prep. Hands-on minutes 5, Walk-away minutes 180, Temperature 300 °F. Critical note left blank. Which lines this step uses: House Beef Stock ticked. Add step is ringed.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s03-add-step-mobile.png "Tick the lines, and the amounts come with them.")

Don't type the amounts into the step. The app says why: "Quantities stay on the line, so scaling reaches the method instead of the prose."

4. Add the rest of the steps the same way. For anything done ahead, tick **Prepare this step the night before**.

![The method: 3 steps, 35 min hands-on, 180 min walk-away, about 3 h 35 min start to finish, and Cook from kitchen view. Step 1 sear the short rib, 20 min hands-on, with Beef short rib, boneless and Mirepoix Base. Step 2 braise, 300°F, 5 min hands-on then 180 min walk-away, with House Beef Stock. Step 3 cool overnight, 10 min hands-on.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s04-method-mobile.png "CostCook adds up the hands-on and walk-away time.")

5. Press **Publish version 1**. Before you publish, read **What this reaches**: the menus that use the dish, the draft orders that will recost, and the confirmed orders that won't change.

![What this reaches: Menus (2), Holiday Party Buffet and Wedding Plated Dinner. Draft orders that will recost (5): Rodriguez product launch #750, Kowalski wedding #752, Alvarez baby shower #757, Salazar memorial luncheon #773, Delacroix company offsite #777. 91 confirmed orders will not change. A confirmed order keeps the costs and quantities it was confirmed with.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s06-reaches-mobile.png "Confirmed orders keep the version they were confirmed with.")

6. Under **What changed, in a few words**, write a short note, then press **Publish version 1**. That note is what the version history shows.

![What changed, in a few words: Wrote the method. This is what history will show beside version 1. Publish version 1 is ringed, beside Keep editing.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s05-publish-mobile.png "A few words now save a guess later.")

## Part 2: Print the sheet

7. Back on the recipe, it reads **Version 1 · Live**: "The kitchen cooks from this version." Press **Kitchen view**.

![The recipe header: Braised Short Rib, Version 1 · Live. The kitchen cooks from this version. Used in 2 menus and 5 draft orders. Kitchen view is ringed, beside Edit recipe.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s07-live-mobile.png "Kitchen view is the cook's sheet.")

8. Set how many you're cooking for. Press **Other…**, type the number, and press **Cook for**.

![The Portions box: 150, and Cook for 150 ringed.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s08-cook-for-mobile.png "The sheet's amounts follow the portions.")

9. Press the printer button at the top of the kitchen view. Your browser's print window opens.

![The kitchen view for 150: Braised Short Rib, Version 1 · published Oct 13, 2026 · 35 min hands-on, then 180 min braise. The printer button is ringed. Mise en place for 150: Beef short rib, boneless 69.5 lb, Mirepoix Base 19.9 lb, House Beef Stock 4.8 gal.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s09-print-button-mobile.png "Set the portions, then print.")

Here's what comes out. The top line says which version it is and the day it was published. Under it are the mise en place for your count, in your kitchen's units, then each step with its amounts and temperature. Steps done ahead get a "The night before" box. A critical note prints after **Check:**. There are no prices on it, and none of the screen's tap-to-finish or timer text.

![The printed sheet: Braised Short Rib. Version 1 · published Oct 13, 2026 · 35 min hands-on, then 180 min braise. Mise en place for 150 with tick boxes: Beef short rib, boneless 69.5 lb, Mirepoix Base 19.9 lb and House Beef Stock 4.8 gal, the last two marked Sub-recipe. Allergens not confirmed. The night before: cool the short rib in its liquid overnight. Method: step 1 with Beef short rib 69.5 lb and Mirepoix Base 19.9 lb; step 2 at 300°F with House Beef Stock 4.8 gal, then 3 h braise; step 3 with Check: Cool it uncovered in shallow pans before it goes in the walk-in.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s10-printed-sheet-mobile.png "Version 1, published Oct 13. The sheet says how old it is.")

**Tip:** need a sheet with the costs on it, for the office? Open **More actions** on the recipe and choose **Print recipe**. That one has a cost column and the plate cost. Both sheets name the version and the day it was published.

![The Print recipe page: Harbor & Hearth Catering. Braised Short Rib. Dish · Quantities are per portion. Version 1 · published Oct 13, 2026.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/tip-costed-print-mobile.png "The costed sheet, for whoever prices the job.")

## Part 3: Change it without confusing the kitchen

10. To change the recipe, press **Edit recipe**, then **Start working copy**. The kitchen keeps cooking version 1 while you work.

![Start a working copy of version 1? A working copy lets you change this recipe privately. The kitchen keeps cooking version 1 until you publish the changes. Publish version 2 when it is ready. Discard working copy to keep version 1 as it is. Confirmed orders always keep the version they were confirmed with.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s11-working-copy-v1-mobile.png "Your edits stay private until you publish.")

![The recipe header while editing: Version 1 · Live, working copy in progress. The kitchen still sees version 1. Your edits stay here until you publish them.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s12-still-v1-mobile.png "Saved isn't published.")

11. Press **Publish version 2**. This time **What changed** lists the difference, and **What publishing does** says what it touches.

![Publish: This replaces version 1 with version 2. Version 1 stays readable in history. What changed: Added Step 4: Reheat covered at 300°F. Reduce the… What publishing does: Nothing the kitchen buys or pays changes. 2 menus and 5 draft orders read the new instructions.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s13-what-changed-mobile.png "A method change doesn't change what you buy.")

12. Print the new sheet, and take the old one off the wall.

13. To see every version, open **More actions**, then **Versions**. Each version keeps its note and date. **Restore version 1 as draft** opens the old one as draft changes for you to review. It never rewrites history.

![Braised Short Rib versions: Every published version is kept as it was. Version 2, Live now, Oct 13, 2026: Added the reheat step. Version 1, Oct 13, 2026: Wrote the method. Read version 2, Read version 1, Restore version 1 as draft.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/s14-history-mobile.png "Every published version is kept as it was.")

**Tip:** keep the critical note for the check that matters, like cooling or holding. Whatever you type there prints after **Check:**.

## If something goes wrong

### "has no saved kitchen version yet"

The recipe has never been published, so there's no kitchen sheet yet. Publish a version (steps 5 and 6).

![This recipe is in the book, but has no saved kitchen version yet. Open the recipe to read it; the owner can review and publish a version.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/fix-no-version-mobile.png "No published version, no kitchen sheet.")

### "older version, not live"

Someone opened an old version. Press **Go to version 2** (or whichever is live) and print from there.

![Braised Short Rib, Version 1 · published Oct 13, 2026 · older version, not live. This is not what the kitchen is cooking. Version 2 is live. Go to version 2.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/fix-old-version-mobile.png "An old sheet says it's old.")

### "Allergens not confirmed"

Nobody has reviewed the allergens on this recipe's ingredients, so the sheet won't say it's clear. Review them first. [How to check a catering menu for a guest's allergy](/blog/guest-allergy-menu-check) walks through it.

![Allergens not confirmed. This recipe's allergens haven't been reviewed. Check the labels before telling a guest what's in it.](/proof/blog/print-recipe-sheet/fix-allergens-mobile.png "Unreviewed isn't clear.")

### The kitchen sheet didn't change

Your edits are in a working copy that hasn't been published. The recipe says "working copy in progress". Press **Publish version 2** to make it the live version.

## Limits

- Amounts on the sheet follow the portions you choose. The step times stay as written.
- Saving a working copy doesn't change the kitchen sheet. Only publishing does.
- A confirmed order keeps the version and costs it was confirmed with. Menus and draft orders move to the new version.
- The kitchen sheet has no prices. **Print recipe** does.
