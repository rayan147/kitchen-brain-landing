The couple said yes on a Sunday night. Great. Monday you're ordering rentals, Tuesday you're holding the date against two other inquiries, and the deposit is still "we'll send it this week." By Friday you've texted twice and you feel like a debt collector for your own wedding.

The deposits you end up chasing are the ones you mentioned and never wrote down.

## Put the money on the proposal, not in a text

Write the deposit into the proposal and the client agrees to it when they agree to the food.

On the proposal, the payment schedule asks how you want to be paid:

- **A deposit, then the balance.** The deposit is **A percentage of the order** or **A fixed amount**. The balance comes **In one payment** or **In several payments**.
- **The full amount, when the client accepts.** For a small job or a new client.
- **Nothing online: you arrange payment with the client.** For the regulars you bill your own way.

**Balance due (days before the event)** starts at 14. The client's final guest count is due the same day as the balance, which is the point: you stop buying for a number that can still move.

While you're writing terms, put in the agreement whether the deposit is refundable, and until when. It's the first thing a couple asks when the date wobbles.

## Work one wedding

An illustrative wedding for 120 guests, accepted at $9,600:

```text
Order total = $9,600
Deposit = 30% × $9,600 = $2,880
Balance = $9,600 − $2,880 = $6,720

Event: Saturday, June 12, 2027
Balance and final count due:
14 days before = Saturday, May 29
```

The $2,880 is what lets you commit before the balance lands: the rentals, the tent deposit, the staff you're turning other work down for. The food order waits for the final count on May 29, and the balance pays for it.

## What the client does

They open the proposal on their phone and choose **Accept the reviewed selection**, **Request changes** or **Decline this offer**. If they accept, the agreement goes out with **Send for signatures**. If they'd rather sign on paper, there's a path for that too.

Then the deposit. On the event, **Ask for a deposit** and pick **How you will take it**:

- **A card, by email.** The amount comes from the agreement, and they get a link. They pay on Stripe's page into your own Stripe account. Stripe takes the card and emails them the receipt.
- **A check, cash or a transfer**, or **My own card processor**. You type the amount, and you record it when it comes in.

Once you've asked, the amount is fixed. If it's wrong, fix it before you send.

## Book on all four, not on a feeling

CostCook won't book on the yes alone. It books the event when four things are true: the proposal was accepted, the agreement is signed by everyone, the deposit came in, and the day still has room.

Until then **Book the event** tells you what's missing, in plain words: "The agreement is not signed by everyone yet." or "The $2,880 deposit has not come in yet." If they paid part of it, it says how much came in.

When it's all there, you book. Booking commits the kitchen to the event; confirming the order comes later, when the kitchen plan is ready.

An owner or manager can press **Book anyway** with a reason. Sometimes that's right, like the planner you've worked with for six years. Write the reason down so you remember why in the spring.

## A few things aren't automatic

- **The deposit is asked for by hand.** Nothing reminds the client about the deposit on its own. If it hasn't come in, open the order's payment request and press **Send again**.
- **Balances by card get one reminder.** Three days before the due date the client gets an email with a pay button. Balances you collect yourself don't send email; your Home page shows **Balance due** with **Record payment** instead.
- **Refunds happen in Stripe.** Cancelling an event doesn't refund anyone, and CostCook doesn't send refunds.
- **A changed guest count doesn't re-bill.** If they drop from 120 to 100 after the deposit, the deposit and payments stay as agreed. A change to what they owe is an invoice correction you make on purpose.

Checks you take by hand go in as **Record a payment**, with a reference like "check #204". A recorded payment can't be edited later, so type it once, right.

## The week after the yes

- The proposal named the deposit, the balance and the balance date before they accepted, and the agreement says whether the deposit comes back.
- The agreement is signed and the deposit is in, or you know exactly what's missing.
- You booked when all four were true, or you booked anyway and wrote down why.

The $2,880 is in your account and the date is booked.
