# Menus & Quotes surface brief

## Thesis

The menu the customer chooses should be the menu the kitchen can cost and run. Present Menu Management Software and Catering Quotes as distinct chapters joined by one menu, guest count, and event commitment.

## Audience and job

The primary reader is an independent caterer or working chef-owner who must answer a customer with a defensible per-guest price and then hand the same job to the kitchen.

## Visual world

Stay inside CostCook's established Kitchen Ticket system: warm paper, deep ink, working green, amber commitment signals, serif editorial headlines, fine rules, code-native diagrams, and real product evidence. The page's distinctive motif is a menu ledger becoming a confirmed event timeline.

## First viewport

Use an asymmetrical editorial split. The headline and actions sit at left; a broad menu ledger flows into a narrow Draft → Review → Confirmed quote timeline at right. The relationship must be legible before individual capabilities are introduced.

## Information architecture

Use one overview route with anchored Menu Management and Catering Quotes chapters. Keep the core story visible, place the selected capability inventory and six FAQs in semantic disclosures, then close with the same customer-and-kitchen outcome.

## Claim discipline

Use only the shipped feature register and claim ledger. Do not imply proposals, signatures, deposits, contracts, BEOs, booking, CRM, customer portals, automated customer email, or competitor integrations.

## Mobile behavior

Stack copy before evidence, reflow menu rows into label-value pairs, preserve the timeline order, use deliberate responsive screenshot sources, and prevent page-level or nested horizontal scrolling.

## Finish contract

The page is finished when it shows one menu moving from draft price to frozen event, answers the practical quote objections, routes the existing Menus & Quotes navigation entry here, and passes desktop/mobile verification without duplicate routes or horizontal overflow.
