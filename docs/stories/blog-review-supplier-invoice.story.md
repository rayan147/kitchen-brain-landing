### Step 1 — The Idea

An owner-caterer who wants this week's supplier prices in the recipes but an invoice line can be read correctly and still be matched to the wrong ingredient or pack.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** Stop retyping the invoice.
- **Need:** Confirm the source line, ingredient match, pack conversion, and price difference before committing it.
- **Wound:** A case price entered as a can price that made every sauce look expensive.
- **Flaw:** Treating text extraction as approval.

### Step 3 — The Plot

| Beat | In this guide |
|------|---------------|
| Opening Image | A PDF invoice lands beside an ingredient list that still carries last month's price. |
| Theme Stated | Reading the line is the fast part; deciding what it means is the important part. |
| Set-Up | “Tom paste 6/#10 38.40” contains a product, pack, and case price. |
| Catalyst | The current record says $34.20 per case. |
| Debate | Is $38.40 a case price, can price, or unrelated line? |
| Break into Two | Match the supplier item and verify the 6-can pack before converting. |
| B Story | Every recipe using tomato paste depends on this one decision. |
| Fun and Games | Convert case to can to ounce and compare with the previous unit cost. |
| Midpoint | The 28 oz sauce line rises from $1.44 to $1.61. |
| Bad Guys Close In | OCR confidence cannot prove the pack or ingredient match. |
| All Is Lost | Silent commitment sends one ambiguous line through every recipe and quote. |
| Finale + Final Image | The source is retained, the difference is shown, and only the confirmed price becomes current. |

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader:** “What changed?”, “Is that per case?”, “Which recipes does this touch?”
- **Product:** restrained, transparent, exact. Banned: AI-powered, effortless, magic.

### Step 6 — Dialogue turns

An extracted line becomes a staged claim; shorthand becomes a pack; a case price becomes a unit cost; the unit-cost change becomes a visible recipe effect; approval becomes deliberate.

### Step 7 — Headline / subhead

- **Intention:** Carry this week's price into the recipes.
- **Obstacle:** Correctly read text can still mean the wrong product, pack, or unit.
- **Headline:** Let the invoice show what changed. You decide what becomes current.
- **Subhead:** Confirm the ingredient and pack first, then follow one case price through the unit conversion and into every recipe that uses it.

### Step 8 — The one snap line

> OCR can read 38.40. It cannot taste the difference between a case and a can.

### Step 9 — Bringing the scene to life

- **Where:** Laptop beside the receiving clipboard.
- **Sensory detail:** Creased PDF, abbreviated supplier description, the previous price directly underneath.
- **Time:** After the cases are put away, before the next quote.

### Step 10 — Connecting the scenes

- **POV:** Second person.
- **Hand-offs:** read → match → convert → compare → trace → commit.

### Step 11 — Revise and finish

- Preserved higher precision until displayed currency values are rounded.
- Removed any promise that OCR confidence proves business meaning.
- Recalculated the reading time at 200 words per minute.
- **Final image:** The invoice remains beside the new price, and the next recipe cost has a source you can inspect.
