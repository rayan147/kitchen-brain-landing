### Step 1 — The Idea

> A chef-owner who wants help setting up the kitchen and finding what needs attention before Saturday, but will not trust an assistant that hides its sources or changes records on its own.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** Ask one plain kitchen question instead of opening several records.
- **Need:** See which record supports each part of the answer and stay responsible for any change.
- **Wound:** A confident answer built on a missing or stale number.
- **Flaw:** Either avoids asking for help or accepts a polished sentence before checking its evidence.

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words:** “What do you need from me?”, “what needs attention?”, “what came up short?”, “what changed?”, “back to setup”, “show me where that came from”.
- **Product voice:** calm, exact, kitchen-literate. Banned: seamless, magic, learns, autonomous, always, perfect.

### Step 6 — Dialogue

- Hero: suspicion → a bounded promise with authentic proof.
- Video: abstract availability → a visible setup/question/source sequence.
- Setup: leaving the stage feels risky → progress is saved and Back to setup is explicit.
- Questions: “what does it actually do?” → six exact jobs.
- Proof: number → record, calculation and evidence count.
- Guardrails: helpful → safely useful because uncertainty and approval remain visible.
- FAQ: reasonable doubt → precise boundary.
- Closing: another tab to check → one question and a next action.

### Step 7 — Sorkin

- **Intention:** Get help now without losing control of the kitchen record.
- **Obstacle:** The chef has good reason not to trust a fluent answer by itself.
- **Headline:** Ask your kitchen. Check the answer.
- **Subhead:** Sage is available in CostCook, including during setup. It reads the records you have, shows where each answer came from, and waits for your approval before prepared work becomes real.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk

> A missing number is an answer, too.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At a prep table after service, setup open on a laptop and Saturday's order on their mind.
- **What they see / hear / feel:** The setup stage is saved; the room is quiet; one unfamiliar field is holding up the next step.
- **Time of day:** Late enough that another help article feels longer than one direct question.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** Second person throughout.
- **Hand-offs:** checkable answer → choose video, jobs, or guardrails from the orientation links → watch the path → use it during setup → ask one of six jobs → inspect a real answer → understand the boundaries → settle objections → start asking.

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Word count before → after:** 1,430 planned words → 1,073 rendered article words, including the orientation links, video chapters, FAQs, and onward navigation.
- **Claims removed because they could not be shown:** perfect answers, learning the business, memory, autonomous actions, time savings, a seventh tool, provider/model identity.
- **Final Image:** Ask the next kitchen question, check the record underneath it, and keep moving.
