### Step 1 — The Idea

> A chef-owner who wants to turn one confirmed catering order into the shopping, prep, and pack work for the crew, but separate lists drift as the guest count, checks, and real kitchen day move.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want (surface):** Get an 80-guest event bought, cooked, and loaded without rebuilding the plan three times.
- **Need (real):** Give every person the same confirmed event truth, while keeping each working check and exception in its proper stage.
- **Wound (the bad day):** Eight hotel pans were prepped, seven reached the van, and the missing pan surfaced at the venue.
- **Flaw (the habit):** She trusts the latest printed sheet, the group chat, and what she remembers as if they were one plan.

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words for the problem:** “guest count,” “what to buy,” “prep list,” “pack list,” “left to grab,” “hotel pan,” “confirmed plan.” Source: `src/lib/features.ts`, PRODUCT.md, and established route-family language.
- **Product voice:** calm, exact, kitchen-literate.
- **Banned words:** seamless, powerful, effortless.

### Step 6 — Dialogue (McKee): the turn in each section

- Hero: three documents to reconcile → one confirmed order feeding three jobs.
- Missing pan: “prep was done” → one pack line is still visibly open.
- Confirm: live estimate keeps moving → the event plan and quote are held together.
- Shop: an ingredient need lives in memory → need, on hand, pack size, and packs stay on the line.
- Prep and Pack: departments hand off by assumption → each stage exposes its own remaining work.
- Recovery: changed plans create false checks → stale checks clear and old plans say they are old.
- Closing: van door closes on a guess → it closes on a completed pack record.

### Step 7 — Sorkin: headline / subhead

- **Intention:** Run one confirmed event all the way to the loading door.
- **Obstacle:** Shopping, prep, and pack drift when each is copied into a separate plan.
- **Headline:** One confirmed order. Three working lists. Nothing important left in somebody's head.
- **Subhead:** Turn the same menu and guest count into what to buy, what to prep, and what must reach the van. Confirmation holds that event plan steady behind every check.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk: the one snap line

> Eight hotel pans prepped. Seven in the van is still one pan short.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the loading door between the walk-in and a half-packed van.
- **What they see / hear / feel:** Cold pan rims, a printer-marked prep sheet, the van alarm chiming, and one unchecked line on a phone.
- **Time of day:** Late afternoon, before departure for service.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** Second person (“you”) throughout.
- **Hand-off lines:**
  - Hero → missing pan: “The plan is not finished until the last handoff is visible.”
  - Missing pan → confirm: “That starts by confirming the event everyone is actually running.”
  - Confirm → shop: “Then the first working question is simple: what still needs buying?”
  - Shop → prep and pack: “What leaves the shelf becomes what the kitchen must make and the van must carry.”
  - Prep and pack → recovery: “If the plan changes, the checks must tell the truth too.”
  - Recovery → close: “So the final open line stays visible until somebody resolves it.”

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Revision target:** Keep the primary story under 440 visible words before the optional capability disclosure; shorten internal product language without losing the evidence.
- **Progressive disclosure:** The 21-item shipped-capability inventory stays complete and accessible, but starts collapsed so the seven-scene narrative remains scannable.
- **Claims removed because they couldn't be shown:** guaranteed prevention of missed items, automatic purchasing, automatic inventory accuracy, time-saved claims, and invented customer results.
- **Illustrative-data rule:** Every guest count, line count, completion count, date, and event name in the page evidence is labeled illustrative.
- **Final Image (the CTA sentence):** Close the van on the same plan you priced, shopped, and cooked.
