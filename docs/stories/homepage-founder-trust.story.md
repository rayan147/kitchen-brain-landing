### Step 1 — The Idea

> A kitchen owner wants to know who is accountable for the operational numbers they will trust, but anonymous software language cannot prove the builder understands the work behind them.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** Know who made CostCook and whether they understand a working kitchen.
- **Need:** Connect the founder’s experience to specific product decisions and a reachable person.
- **Wound:** Bought software whose support team knew the interface but not the kitchen problem.
- **Flaw:** Treats a founder biography as proof without checking what it changed in the product.

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words:** who made it, cooked professionally, one menu, shopping list, prep plan, pack list, missing price, ask whether it fits.
- **Founder voice:** first person, accountable, kitchen-literate.
- **Banned:** visionary, passionate, industry-leading, trusted by, team when one named founder is shown.

### Step 6 — Dialogue

- Anonymous product → Rayan Ramirez and a real portrait.
- Generic founder biography → twelve years cooking professionally.
- Experience claim → missing facts stop, one event stays connected, event-production scope remains explicit.
- Support abstraction → a direct Contact Rayan link.

### Step 7 — Sorkin

- **Intention:** Trust the person responsible for the product’s operational decisions.
- **Obstacle:** Kitchen experience is only useful if it changed how the product behaves.
- **Headline:** I cooked professionally for twelve years before I built CostCook.
- **Subhead:** I wanted one menu to become the quote, shopping list, prep plan and pack list without losing the cost on the way.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk

The homepage keeps its established snap line—“The numbers move between them so you do not.”—and this section adds no rival.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the bottom of the page, looking at the person who will answer if the plan does not fit the kitchen.
- **What they see:** A real portrait, full name, twelve years of kitchen experience, and three consequences in the product.
- **Time of day:** While deciding whether to put the next real order through the system.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** Founder testimony in first person inside a page otherwise addressed to “you.”
- **Hand-offs:** Honest comparison → named founder and product decisions → direct accountability → put one real order through it.

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Before → after:** A thumbnail and two feature rows become a portrait-led provenance scene with three explicit product consequences and direct contact.
- **Claims removed:** No testimonials, customer counts, logos, or team-size implication are added.
- **Final image:** The visitor knows Rayan Ramirez made CostCook after twelve professional cooking years and can ask him directly before testing one order.
