### Step 1 — The Idea

> A chef-owner wants to see how one event moves from supplier price to packed order, but six evenly weighted text blocks make the sequence feel like a list instead of a connected job.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** Understand the whole operating loop in one glance.
- **Need:** See what comes next without rereading labels or remembering the previous row.
- **Wound:** Another event where the quote, buy sheet, prep list, and pack notes disagreed.
- **Flaw:** Skims the page between kitchen tasks and will not decode a diagram whose direction disappears on a phone.

### Step 3 — The Plot

| Beat | In this piece |
|------|---------------|
| 1 Opening Image | A supplier price sheet or invoice arrives. |
| 2 Theme Stated | One set of numbers should travel through the whole job. |
| 3 Set-Up | Recipes and menus need costing before a quote can be trusted. |
| 4 Catalyst | The menu and head count become a real order. |
| 5 Debate | Can that order become kitchen work without rebuilding it elsewhere? |
| 6 Break into Two | The order rolls into a supplier-grouped shop list. |
| 7 B Story | The cooks need batches and whole pots, not abstract ingredient totals. |
| 8 Fun and Games | Prep quantities become portions, equipment, and notes to pack. |
| 9 Midpoint | The same numbers have reached the van. |
| 10 Bad Guys Close In | What arrived and what it cost may differ from the plan. |
| 11 All Is Lost | If the actual price is lost, the next quote starts stale again. |
| 12 Finale + Final Image | Receiving closes the loop and feeds the next quote. |

### Step 4 — From Beats to Scenes

| § | Section | Beats | Value turn |
|---|---------|-------|------------|
| 1 | Price in | 1–2 | paper arrives → usable source price |
| 2 | Cost it | 3 | ingredients → recipe and menu cost |
| 3 | Order | 4–5 | proposed menu → head-counted job |
| 4 | Shop | 6 | total need → supplier packs |
| 5 | Prep | 7 | ingredient quantities → kitchen batches |
| 6 | Pack | 8–9 | prepared food → complete event load |
| 7 | Receive | 10–12 | planned price → approved actual price for next time |

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words:** price sheet, invoice, menu, head count, supplier, whole packs, batches, whole pots, portions, equipment, notes.
- **Product voice:** calm, operational, exact.
- **Banned:** seamless, powerful, automated, magic.

### Step 6 — Dialogue

The chain turns disconnected kitchen documents into one visible sequence; the receiving line then turns an ending into the next reliable starting point.

### Step 7 — Sorkin

- **Intention:** Follow one event without rebuilding its numbers.
- **Obstacle:** Each kitchen stage usually lives in a different sheet or person's memory.
- **Headline:** One chain, and it closes.
- **Subhead:** Every step is a screen you already work in. The numbers move between them so you do not.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk

> The numbers move between them so you do not.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the prep table, tracing tomorrow's event from an invoice to a load-out list.
- **What they see / hear / feel:** Supplier paper, a head count, whole-pack buying, pots on the stove, boxes at the door.
- **Time of day:** The narrow planning window before prep begins.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** You, locked in the caption and receiving explanation.
- **Hand-offs:** A continuous arrowed spine connects every stop; the receive path is offset because it returns the result to the next cycle.
