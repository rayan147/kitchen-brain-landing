### Step 1 — The Idea

> A chef-owner who wants to close the month knowing whether food spend matched the food the kitchen should have used, but purchase records arrive through several doors and the remaining gap is too easy to call waste without evidence.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want (surface):** Explain this month's food-cost result before planning the next one.
- **Need (real):** Separate recorded purchases, theoretical use, known waste, and the still-unexplained remainder without rewriting history.
- **Wound (the bad day):** A month looked hundreds over budget, and the kitchen was blamed before late invoices and a missed count were found.
- **Flaw (the habit):** She totals supplier bills, calls the difference waste, and moves on because the source records live in different places.

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words for the problem:** “what we bought,” “what we should have used,” “month cost,” “waste,” “price moved,” “late invoice,” “count.” Source: `src/lib/features.ts`, PRODUCT.md, and established CostCook feature language.
- **Product voice:** calm, exact, kitchen-literate.
- **Banned words:** seamless, powerful, effortless.

### Step 6 — Dialogue (McKee): the turn in each section

- Hero: a month-end total demands a verdict → the difference can be inspected.
- Gap: one number gets called waste → only supported parts receive a label.
- Ledger: four entry paths create four stories → origin stays attached to every row.
- Reconciliation: actual spend and theoretical use disagree → logged waste and the remaining gap separate.
- Dates: a correction rewrites what happened → signed adjustments and as-of-date costs preserve history.
- Trust: every report should produce a metric → turnover waits for two trusted counts and missing evidence stays visible.
- Closing: defend a guess → review a month whose known and unknown parts are named.

### Step 7 — Sorkin: headline / subhead

- **Intention:** Close the month with a food-cost explanation the kitchen can inspect.
- **Obstacle:** Purchases enter several ways, prices move, and the remaining difference is easy to mislabel.
- **Headline:** Know what the month should have cost—and what it actually did.
- **Subhead:** Bring every purchase into one sourced ledger, compare theoretical use with actual spend, and keep the unaccounted gap honest.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk: the one snap line

> A $715 gap is not automatically $715 of waste.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the small office desk beside the kitchen after the last service of the month.
- **What they see / hear / feel:** Supplier PDFs, a marked delivery slip, the hum of the walk-in, and one total that does not explain itself.
- **Time of day:** Late evening, before payroll and next month's buying decisions.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** Second person (“you”) throughout.
- **Hand-off lines:**
  - Hero → gap: “First, refuse the easy label.”
  - Gap → ledger: “Then put every purchase behind the number.”
  - Ledger → reconciliation: “Once the sources agree on the month, compare what came in with what the kitchen should have used.”
  - Reconciliation → dates: “The explanation holds only if dates and corrections still tell the truth.”
  - Dates → trust: “When the evidence is not strong enough, the report should say so.”
  - Trust → close: “So the month ends with a review, not a guess.”

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Word count before → after:** 760-word draft → 897 rendered article words, including evidence labels, recovery guidance, FAQs, and onward navigation. The earlier under-450 target is retired because it did not count the supporting copy required to keep the month result inspectable.
- **Claims removed because they couldn't be shown:** guaranteed waste reduction, automatic blame assignment, universal accounting reconciliation, savings, and time outcomes.
- **Illustrative-data rule:** Every amount, percentage, date, supplier, item, and monthly variance used in the page evidence is labeled illustrative.
- **Final Image (the CTA sentence):** Close the month with every known cost named—and every open question still visible.
