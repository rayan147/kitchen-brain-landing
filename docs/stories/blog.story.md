### Step 1 — The Idea

> A working chef-owner wants a trustworthy answer while pricing or running a real job, but the useful calculation is usually scattered across supplier cases, recipe sheets, spreadsheets, and memory.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** Answer the question in front of them: what to charge, buy, prep, or check.
- **Need:** See the working well enough to use it again when a price or guest count moves.
- **Wound:** A quote that looked profitable until the invoice arrived, or a prep morning made expensive by a hidden assumption.
- **Flaw:** Keeps the real adjustment in their head because rebuilding the sheet takes longer than guessing.

### Step 3 — The Plot

| Beat | In this piece |

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words:** per head, case price, usable yield, short delivery, the count changed, what do I charge.
- **Product voice:** calm, exact, kitchen-literate.
- **Banned:** seamless, powerful, effortless, hacks, secrets.

### Step 6 — Dialogue

The index turns “another software blog” into “the answer to the kitchen question I have now.” Each article turns an apparently simple number into visible working, then returns it to the reader as a decision.

### Step 7 — Sorkin

- **Intention:** Get a usable answer before the next quote, order, or prep list is due.
- **Obstacle:** The relevant facts live in different places and change at different times.
- **Headline:** Practical answers for the numbers behind the food.
- **Subhead:** Cost a recipe, price a menu, plan the buy, and keep the working visible when the count or supplier price changes.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk

> The guest count changed once. Your five spreadsheets changed separately.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where:** At the prep table after service, laptop open beside tomorrow's function sheet.
- **What:** The latest supplier invoice, an old recipe yield, and a customer waiting for a revised number.
- **When:** Late enough that rebuilding the workbook feels slower than guessing.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** Second person throughout.
- **Hand-off:** Every article ends with the next operational question, a related feature explanation, and one appropriate action.

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- Cut generic restaurant advice, trend commentary, unsupported savings, and invented outcomes.
- Keep every example explicitly illustrative unless it is drawn from approved product evidence.
- **Final image:** You close the article with the number worked through and the next kitchen decision named.
