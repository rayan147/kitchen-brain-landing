### Step 1 — The Idea

An owner-caterer who wants to put tomorrow's delivery away but the case count, pack size, and invoice no longer agree with the purchase order.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** Accept the good food and get the driver on the road.
- **Need:** Preserve what actually arrived before any stock or recipe price changes.
- **Wound:** The substitution that looked harmless and left the kitchen short during prep.
- **Flaw:** Counting cases instead of checking the item, pack, quantity, condition, and price separately.

### Step 3 — The Plot

| Beat | In this guide |
|------|---------------|
| Opening Image | Six cases are at the back door and the driver is holding the invoice. |
| Theme Stated | “Six delivered” is not an answer when one of the six is a different case. |
| Set-Up | Staff normally count boxes, initial the paper, and discover differences later. |
| Catalyst | Five ordered cases and one smaller substitute arrive, but the invoice total is higher. |
| Debate | Should the kitchen reject everything, accept everything, or fix it later? |
| Break into Two | Check each received line against the order before committing it. |
| B Story | The prep cook tomorrow needs pounds of the right chicken, not today's case count. |
| Fun and Games | Separate quantity, specification, condition, and price; record the substitute distinctly. |
| Midpoint | The arithmetic shows 230 lb arrived, not the 240 lb ordered. |
| Bad Guys Close In | The substitute may still be usable, but it must not inherit the original pack or price. |
| All Is Lost | Accepting “6” silently makes the shelf, recipe cost, and next order wrong. |
| Finale + Final Image | Five original cases and one reviewed substitute are put away with the shortage visible. |

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader:** “Is it all here?”, “Can I use this?”, “The driver's waiting.”
- **Product:** calm, exact, practical. Banned: seamless, smart, optimize.

### Step 6 — Dialogue turns

Hurry becomes attention; a case count becomes a line check; a confusing substitution becomes a visible variance; acceptance becomes an explicit decision; the shelf becomes trustworthy.

### Step 7 — Headline / subhead

- **Intention:** Put the delivery away.
- **Obstacle:** What arrived is not what was ordered.
- **Headline:** The delivery arrived wrong. Check it before “received” becomes the new truth.
- **Subhead:** Put the purchase order, the cases, and the invoice side by side. Accept the good food without carrying a bad quantity or price into tomorrow's prep.

### Step 8 — The one snap line

> Six boxes can still be five cases and a problem.

### Step 9 — Bringing the scene to life

- **Where:** Back door, pallet half in the kitchen.
- **Sensory detail:** Cold cardboard, idling truck, pen clipped to an invoice.
- **Time:** The last delivery before afternoon prep.

### Step 10 — Connecting the scenes

- **POV:** Second person.
- **Hand-offs:** Each section ends on the next decision: compare → calculate → inspect → record → commit.
