### Step 1 — The Idea

> An owner-chef who wants to know whether CostCook fits the work they actually run, but “catering software” sounds narrower than a kitchen that also runs restaurant service, special dinners, or changing menus.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want (surface):** A quick, honest answer to “is this for a kitchen like mine?”
- **Need (real):** To judge fit from how work moves, not from a business-category label.
- **Wound (the bad day):** A dated menu changes and the quote, buying, prep, and pack plan split into separate versions.
- **Flaw (the habit):** Self-rejecting because “restaurant,” “meal prep,” or “private dinner” is not the category named first.

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words for the problem:** “I run a restaurant too.” “We do special dinners.” “Our menu changes for every event.” “Does this still fit?”
- **Product voice:** plainspoken, kitchen-literate, direct. **Banned:** misfit, wrong-shape, restaurant-only.

### Step 6 — Dialogue (McKee)

- “The category excludes me” → “The work pattern includes me.”
- “Restaurant service and events are different businesses” → “They can share one kitchen while keeping different plans.”
- “I need the right persona badge” → “I can recognize my work in the fit test.”
- “Every change means re-keying” → “The menu and guest count drive the downstream plan.”
- “A fit page will hide the catch” → “The three unsupported requirements are named.”
- “I need another sales claim” → “I can test one real menu and guest count.”

### Step 7 — Sorkin: headline / subhead

- **Intention:** Decide quickly whether this product belongs in the reader's kitchen.
- **Obstacle:** “Catering” is heard as a business category instead of an operating pattern.
- **Headline:** The work decides whether CostCook fits. Not the sign out front.
- **Subhead:** If you plan changing menus by date and guest count, CostCook is built for that work—whether you run catering, meal prep, special dinners, a restaurant, or more than one of them.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk: the one snap line

> The same kitchen can run Tuesday service and Saturday's private dinner.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the kitchen pass with the coming week's service and event tickets side by side.
- **What they see / hear / feel:** One recurring menu, one dated guest-count menu, the same cooks and suppliers, and the relief of not having to choose one business label.
- **Time of day:** Late afternoon, before the next order and prep decisions are made.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** You, locked throughout.
- **Hand-offs:** “Look at the calendar, not the category.” → “Now name the work that repeats.” → “When the guest count moves, the plan has somewhere to go.” → “Fit also means knowing where it stops.” → “Take one real menu into the decision.”

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Word count before → after:** 640 planned → 362 rendered words
- **Claims removed because they couldn't be shown:** Any promise that CostCook runs POS, table service, every restaurant workflow, labor margin, or enterprise controls.
- **Final Image:** Bring one real menu and guest count. See whether the work fits.
