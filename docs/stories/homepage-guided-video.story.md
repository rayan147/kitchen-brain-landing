### Step 1 — The Idea

> A working chef-owner who wants to see whether one order really stays connected from quote to received price, but cannot reliably follow two and a half minutes of silent, fast-moving product footage.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want (surface):** Understand what the film proves without pausing and scrubbing through it.
- **Need (real):** Trace one event through every operational handoff and see where the same facts survive.
- **Wound (the bad day):** A polished demo jumped between screens so quickly that the claim sounded connected even though the work was not.
- **Flaw (the habit):** Skips video when sound is off, time is short, motion is difficult to track, or the connection between cuts is unclear.

### Step 3 — The Plot (12 beats)

| Beat | In this piece |
|------|---------------|
| 1 Opening Image — life before | The visitor sees a 2½-minute film and cannot tell whether they will be able to follow its cuts. |
| 2 Theme Stated — the truth they'll learn | The same order should carry its facts from the price you quote to the price that reaches the shelf. |
| 3 Set-Up — the daily grind, the flaw on display | Quote, shopping, prep, purchasing, receiving, and later pricing usually need separate explanations. |
| 4 Catalyst — the bad day / the wall hits | A delivery arrives different from the purchase order, testing whether the chain is real. |
| 5 Debate — “can it be different?” | Is this working software, or are disconnected screens being edited into a story? |
| 6 Break into Two — they try the new way | Start with one 180-guest wedding and read the six handoffs in order. |

| 8 Fun and Games — the promise of the premise | Cost before quote; Shop and Prep from the order; purchase orders sent; delivery checked. |
| 9 Midpoint — first real win, with a number | One 180-guest event reaches the shelf without changing identity. |
| 10 Bad Guys Close In — the edge cases, the doubts | The received quantity and price do not match what was ordered. |

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words:** quote, food cost, shopping list, prep list, purchase order, what arrived, current price, three events, one run.
- **Product voice:** calm, literal, sequential. **Banned:** seamless, magic, automated, watch now, mockup-only superlatives.

### Step 6 — Dialogue (McKee): the turn in each section

1. “I cannot follow the film” → “I can read the same path.”
2. The quote is a guess → the food cost is checked before it goes out.
3. The order is just a sale → it becomes the kitchen's shopping and prep work.
4. A list stops at paper → supplier purchase orders can be sent from the job.
5. The delivery is assumed right → the short or changed line is recorded.
6. Receiving is a dead end → the received price returns to costing.
7. One-event proof feels narrow → three events stay separate while production is planned together.

### Step 7 — Sorkin: headline / subhead

- **Intention:** Let the visitor verify the full operational chain without depending on video.
- **Obstacle:** The film moves through several screens in two and a half minutes.
- **Headline:** Follow the order without pressing play.
- **Subhead:** Read the same 180-guest wedding in six handoffs, then see what changes when three events share one production run.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk: the one snap line

> The page keeps its existing, evidence-backed snap line in the pricing proof: “Charge at least $89.78 per guest to meet the 30% target.” This companion adds no competing snap line.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the back door with the purchase order open and a delivery line that does not match.
- **What they see / hear / feel:** Cases on the floor, a received quantity and price beside the expected line, and the relief of being able to record what actually arrived.
- **Time of day:** During receiving, before the next menu is costed.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** Second person throughout.
- **Hand-offs:** Check the cost → make the work → send the buy → record the delivery → carry the price back → combine the next three events.
- The reading path sits next to the film in the same section and ends before the existing next-section handoff.

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Word count before → after:** Initial seven-step draft 228 words → final target under 170 words.
- **Claims removed because they couldn't be shown:** No time-saved, margin-improvement, error-reduction, or automatic-confirmation claims. The multi-event step says planning only; confirmation and purchase orders remain per order.
- **Final Image:** You can follow one order all the way through, then see three events stay separate while the kitchen plans the run together.
