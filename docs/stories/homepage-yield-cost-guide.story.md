### Step 1 — The Idea

> A kitchen owner wants to know what an ingredient really costs in a recipe, but the supplier sells a case that still includes skins and trim the recipe never uses.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** See the ingredient cost that belongs on the recipe.
- **Need:** Follow one purchase from case price through usable yield and recipe quantity without doing the conversion by hand.
- **Wound:** A recipe looked profitable until prep waste and pack conversions appeared on the invoice.
- **Flaw:** Divides the case price by the purchased weight and treats every ounce as usable.

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words:** used in recipe, trim yield, required to buy, current purchase cost, line cost, cored and wedged. These are the labels in the shipped calculation disclosure.
- **Product voice:** kitchen-literate, exact, calm.
- **Banned:** waste factor, optimization, seamless.

### Step 6 — Dialogue

- Case label → usable recipe quantity.
- Usable quantity → the as-purchased amount the kitchen must buy.
- Purchase cost → the exact share charged to the recipe line.
- Screenshot → proof that the same working remains open in the product.

### Step 7 — Sorkin

- **Intention:** Trust the ingredient cost on the recipe.
- **Obstacle:** The purchased case includes trim the plate never receives.
- **Headline:** The case has the skins on it. Your recipe does not.
- **Subhead:** Follow one tomato from 60 grams used, through 91 percent yield and 66 grams bought, to its 17-cent line cost.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk

The homepage already owns one snap line—“The numbers move between them so you do not.”—so this section adds no rival.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the prep table, with a cored tomato on the board and the case price on the invoice.
- **What they see:** 60 grams in the recipe, 66 grams required from the case, and 17 cents on the line.
- **Time of day:** While costing the recipe before the order is quoted.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** You, locked throughout.
- **Hand-offs:** The outcome section promises costs that move with the work; this section opens one line and shows the movement; nutrition follows from that same costed recipe.

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Before → after:** Two examples and an abstract explanation become one tomato and one visible equation.
- **Claims removed:** “Most sheets add ten percent” is removed because the section does not need a category claim to explain CostCook's behavior.
- **Final image:** You can read 17 cents on the recipe line and trace it back through the 66 grams bought to the 60 grams the dish uses.
