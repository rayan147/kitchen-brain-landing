# Story Tracker — Catering menu pricing

- **Idea:** A chef-owner wants to choose a selling price, but markup, margin, and food-cost percentage are being used as though they mean the same thing.
- **Character:** Wants one price; needs the target translated; remembers a busy low-margin season; reaches for a familiar multiplier.
- **Plot:** Three terms → one example → inverse formulas → limits → a price the reader can defend.
- **Scenes:** Name the terms; show the same menu three ways; calculate; include non-food costs; choose; review.
- **Voice:** Second person; plain, measured, specific; no perfect-price promises.
- **Dialogue:** Conflicting pricing language becomes one explicit target and its consequence.
- **Headline:** Catering menu pricing: margin, markup, and food-cost target
- **Snap:** Thirty percent food cost is not a 30% markup.
- **Life:** A quote is open and three calculators return three different answers.
- **Connection:** The chosen target hands off to recipe and menu costing.
