### Step 1 — The Idea

> A kitchen owner wants to know whether CostCook works with their real numbers, but another polished demo cannot test their supplier prices, yields, menu and guest count.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want:** Try one real order without turning evaluation into a software project.
- **Need:** See the complete path from menu to cost, shopping, prep and pack using familiar kitchen facts.
- **Wound:** A tool looked convincing with demo data and fell apart on the first real job.
- **Flaw:** Keeps evaluating feature lists instead of testing the order already on the calendar.

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words:** one real order, menu, guest count, current supplier prices, food cost, shopping list, prep sheet, pack list, cancel, day sixteen.
- **Product voice:** direct, specific, unhurried.
- **Banned:** effortless, instant setup, no-risk, free without the card boundary.

### Step 6 — Dialogue

- Demo → one familiar order.
- Inputs → menu, guest count and supplier prices.
- Outputs → food cost, shopping, prep and pack.
- Card anxiety → today, day sixteen, cancellation and export stated before the action.
- Continued evaluation → Start CostCook or book the existing short demo.

### Step 7 — Sorkin

- **Intention:** Find out whether the product holds up on the next real job.
- **Obstacle:** Demo numbers cannot expose gaps in the reader’s own prices, yields and conversions.
- **Headline:** Put one real order through it.
- **Subhead:** Bring the menu and guest count already on your calendar. Check the food cost, buy, prep and pack plan it gives back before you move the rest of the kitchen.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk

The homepage keeps its established snap line—“The numbers move between them so you do not.”—and this close adds no rival.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the desk after the quote went out, with the menu and supplier prices still open.
- **What they see:** One guest count feeding four pieces of usable kitchen work.
- **Time of day:** Before the order goes out and before prep starts.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** You, locked throughout.
- **Hand-offs:** Founder accountability → your own order as the test → visible inputs and outputs → card boundary → one existing start action.

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Before → after:** A paragraph names the outputs; the redesign draws the three inputs and four returned plans in one scan, while moving the trial terms into a decision ticket.
- **Claims removed:** None. Cancellation, recipe export, day-sixteen billing and the missing ingredient-price export remain stated at their existing boundaries.
- **Final image:** You start with the next menu on the calendar and decide from your own food cost and kitchen plan.
