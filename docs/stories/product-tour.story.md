# Story Tracker — Product tour

## My story

- **Piece:** Guided product tour
- **Title / headline:** One event. Every part of the week that gets it out the door.
- **My hero's name (the reader, as a person):** Alex, a chef-owner pricing and running a 180-guest wedding
- **Content file(s):** `src/pages/tour/main.astro`, `src/components/sections/ProductTour.astro`, `src/lib/tour.ts`

### Step 1 — The Idea

> A chef-owner who wants to price and run one 180-guest wedding from the same plan, but the working facts are usually split across recipes, supplier files, shopping notes, and month-end records.

### Step 2 — Your Character

- **Want (surface):** See whether CostCook covers the whole working week before starting a trial.
- **Need (real):** Trust that one event can carry the same facts from quote through close-out.
- **Wound (the bad day):** The quote was built from an old price, the prep list was copied by hand, and a short delivery appeared only when service was close.
- **Flaw (the habit):** Alex checks each tool separately and fills the gaps from memory.

### Step 3 — The Plot (12 beats)

| Beat | In this piece |
|------|---------------|
| 1 Opening Image — life before | Alex has a wedding due Saturday and the facts for it live in different places. |
| 2 Theme Stated — the truth they'll learn | The plan you price should be the plan the kitchen runs. |
| 3 Set-Up — the daily grind, the flaw on display | Recipe, case price, quote, shopping list, delivery, and month cost each need another check. |
| 4 Catalyst — the bad day / the wall hits | A 180-guest commitment turns one stale fact into a real shortfall. |
| 5 Debate — “can it be different?” | Alex needs to see the product, not another list of claims. |
| 6 Break into Two — they try the new way | The guided tour opens on the costed herb roast chicken used in the event. |
| 7 B Story — the person/relationship it's really about | The kitchen crew gets the same current plan the owner used to quote. |
| 8 Fun and Games — the promise of the premise | Twelve selectable stops follow the event through every feature in the dropdown, including the Coming label workflow and the real Owner, Manager, and Staff boundaries. |
| 9 Midpoint — first real win, with a number | The illustrative event is 180 guests at $28 per guest, with $1,491.38 food cost shown. |

| 11 All Is Lost / Dark Night — the risk if nothing changes | Without one record, Alex discovers the bad handoff after the promise is already made. |
| 12 Finale + Final Image — life after; the CTA in their words | Start with one real menu and carry it through the same twelve stops. |

### Step 5 — Character Voices

- **Reader's words for the problem:** “what does this plate actually cost,” “what do I need to buy,” “what arrived,” “where did that answer come from,” “can I see the math.” These come from `PRODUCT.md`, the shipped feature register, and existing feature-page language.
- **Product voice:** competent, calm, kitchen-literate. Banned: seamless, powerful, effortless.

### Step 6 — Dialogue (McKee): the turn in each section

- Hero: “I have twelve disconnected jobs” → “they can follow one event.”
- Orientation: “show me claims” → “let me choose a real product stop.”
- Build and price: “the number is assembled elsewhere” → “the path from case to quote stays visible.”
- Run the event: “lists are copied” → “shop, prep, pack, PO, and receiving read the same commitment.”
- Close the loop: “month end is blame” → “the gap and its evidence are named.”
- Completion: “I need another explanation” → “I can start with my own menu.”

### Step 7 — Sorkin: headline / subhead

- **Intention:** See the whole product through one event.
- **Obstacle:** Feature lists hide the handoffs between jobs.
- **Headline:** One event. Every part of the week that gets it out the door.
- **Subhead:** Follow a fully seeded 180-guest wedding from the working recipe and customer quote through buying, receiving, inventory, labels, month cost, and Sage.

### Step 8 — Cool Talk: the one snap line

> The same 7.8 kg of chicken has to survive the recipe, the purchase order, and the back door.

### Step 9 — Bringing a Scene to Life

- **Where they are:** At the prep table before the Saturday event, opening the plan on a laptop and then checking it on a phone.
- **What they see / hear / feel:** A current case price, a 180-guest count, the short line on the delivery, and a source link behind the answer.
- **Time of day:** The planning week turns into the five-in-the-morning receiving check.

### Step 10 — Connecting Your Scenes

- **POV:** Second person throughout.
- **Hand-offs:** Recipe cost hands to menu price; nutrition hands its confirmed facts to the Coming label workflow; menu hands to order; order hands to PO; receiving hands to inventory; purchases hand to the month verdict; Team & access establishes the human boundary before Sage checks the connected records; completion hands to the visitor's own menu.

### Step 11 — Revise and Finish

- **Word count before → after:** 1,230 → 760 words across the route and guided stops after the Labels & printing integration.
- **Claims removed because they couldn't be shown:** No ROI, adoption, time-saved, accuracy, customer, or implementation-speed claims.
- **Final Image:** Start with one real menu. Keep it intact all the way to the back door.
