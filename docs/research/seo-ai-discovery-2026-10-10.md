### Content that can earn useful visibility

Google's current generative AI guide emphasizes information based on first-hand experience or original work that could not easily be reproduced from other pages, supported by clear sections and relevant images or video. It explicitly advises against creating a page for every query variation or rewriting pages solely for AI systems. Bing likewise says to align content with user intent, support claims with evidence, and keep material accurate and current. For CostCook, this suggests publicly accessible, factual pages showing real kitchen workflows, sample calculations with stated assumptions, clear product scope, pricing, and specific comparisons where the team can substantiate them. The CostCook application of this guidance is an **inference**, not a promise that those pages will be cited. [Google 2026 AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) · [Bing AI Performance guidance](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/ai-performance-9f8e7d6c)

Google says it primarily generates snippets from page content and may use the meta description when it better describes the page. Unique, accurate page descriptions help users evaluate a result. Crawlable `<a href>` links with descriptive anchor text help Google find and understand pages. These are more defensible priorities than adding repetitive keywords to metadata. [Google snippets guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet) · [Google links guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) · [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

### Structured data to keep and assess

Use schema only where it matches visible, truthful page content. Google's `Organization` guidance recommends a home or about page to clarify the business entity. Its `SoftwareApplication` rich result requires `name`, `offers.price`, **and a genuine rating or review**; CostCook has no approved customer review evidence in this repo, so it should not add a fabricated rating to qualify. An applicable article may use `Article` markup. Google does not guarantee a rich result even when markup validates. Its FAQ rich results have been restricted to well-known government and health sites, so `FAQPage` markup on a SaaS marketing site should not be treated as a visibility lever. [Organization structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization) · [Software app structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app) · [Structured data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) · [Google FAQ change notice](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes)

## CostCook observations and prioritized next work

The observations in this section came from the repository and live site check on 2026-10-10; they are **not** platform claims. The checked `develop` revision was `ab176ed`. Live `costcook.io` appears ahead of that revision: live sitemap lists 17 blog articles, while `develop` has 11 guides. Recheck both deployment and source before changing anything. The live home page returned HTTP 200; `www` redirected to the apex; `robots.txt` allowed crawling and advertised `sitemap-index.xml`. The repo already has canonical URLs, per-route descriptions, Open Graph metadata in `Base.astro`, `@astrojs/sitemap`, `Article` markup on blog pages, and `FAQPage` markup. The home page currently says “Catering software, built by a chef” and “Know what the job makes before you cook it.” Existing guides include real food-cost-per-guest, case-to-portion, menu-pricing, and whole-pack calculations, with Rayan Ramirez credited as author. This means the first pass should verify correctness and gaps rather than duplicate existing plumbing.

The affected visitor journey is search result → relevant guide or feature page → assess proof and pricing → start trial or book demo. The immediate screen is the organic landing page; the key frame is its result title/snippet and first viewport; the action is proceeding to trial or demo. Existing routes can support that journey. Current evidence does not justify route or workflow restructuring.

1. **Establish baselines in owner tools.** Verify the preferred `https://costcook.io` property in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools; submit the current XML sitemap; inspect representative home, product, pricing, and article URLs for crawl, index, selected canonical, and rendered content. Google recommends URL Inspection and Search Console for diagnosis. A single `site:` query is inconclusive because even an indexed URL is not guaranteed to appear in such results. [Google developer guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started-developers) · [Google `site:` operator guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-operators/all-search-site)
2. **Align sitemap URLs and canonical URLs.** The live sitemap lists trailing-slash URLs such as `/blog/food-cost-per-guest/`, while that page's HTML canonical omits the trailing slash. Both variants currently return HTTP 200. Choose one URL form, then align sitemap, internal links, canonicals, and ideally redirects. This is a cleanup for consistent canonical signals; it is **not** evidence of the primary indexing cause. Google says a sitemap URL is a weaker canonical signal than redirects and `rel=canonical`, and recommends linking internally to the preferred URL. [Google canonical guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls)
3. **Clean sitemap and indexability intent.** The live `sitemap-0.xml` contains roughly 50 URLs, including `/contact/sent/`, `/contact/not-sent/`, `/demo/sent/`, and `/demo/not-sent/`. `/demo/sent/` has a self canonical and no observed `noindex`. Verify that these are utility states. If so, remove them from the sitemap and apply `noindex` while keeping the useful no-JavaScript flows accessible to users; do not use `robots.txt` as the indexing control because crawlers need to read the `noindex` rule. Keep the sitemap to canonical, useful destination pages and use accurate `lastmod` values when maintained. [Google sitemap guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap) · [Google noindex guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing) · [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)
4. **Check all crawler and infrastructure layers.** The permissive live `robots.txt` is only one layer. Check actual 200 responses and rendered text for `Googlebot`, `Bingbot`, `OAI-SearchBot`, `Claude-SearchBot`, and `Claude-User`, along with CDN/WAF challenges and server logs. Keep search versus model-training crawler decisions explicit: `OAI-SearchBot` and `Claude-SearchBot` serve search discovery, whereas `GPTBot` and `ClaudeBot` relate to training. [OpenAI crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) · [Anthropic crawler guidance](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler) · [Google developer guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/get-started-developers)
5. **Make the product and brand unambiguous on high-value pages.** Search results surfaced a separate `costcook.app` product during an initial brand query. A separate exploratory query for independent-caterer software returned competitors such as NXCatering, Catertoo, LightCater, iCOST PRO, and Better Cater, but not `costcook.io`. These are limited query snapshots, **not** proof of ranking or index coverage. The product brief targets independent caterers and restaurant owners with catering, while the default home title says “CostCook: catering from first inquiry to closeout.” Audit page titles, visible headings, logo/organization data, product description, and canonical domain consistency so a reader can distinguish CostCook's catering and kitchen cost workflows. Add clear, real examples only where the product supports them. This is a site-specific **inference** from Google's advice on descriptive titles, unique content, and organization disambiguation. [Google title link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link) · [Organization structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization) · [Google 2026 AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
6. **Review existing schema against real page claims.** Retain valid `Article` markup. Review `FAQPage` markup for accuracy but do not expand it just for Google rich results. Evaluate `Organization` on the homepage. Defer `SoftwareApplication` rich-result markup until a genuine review or rating is available alongside the real offer and price; validate any eventual markup with Google's Rich Results Test. [Organization structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization) · [Software app structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app) · [Structured data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies)
7. **Choose priority buyer queries and market, then extend existing evidence-led content.** The target geography and primary query family remain assumptions pending owner input. “Catering workflow software” and “recipe/food-cost software” may attract different buyers, so choose a primary query set and market before rewriting or publishing. Map Search Console and Bing queries to specific questions from caterers and kitchen operators, then extend the existing calculation guides or publish a limited set of substantive pages built from firsthand calculations, screenshots, workflows, and qualifications. Avoid mass-producing near-identical query pages or generic AI summaries. This is an **inference** applying Google's non-commodity content and scaled-content guidance to CostCook. ## Measurement and decision rules

- **Google:** Track indexed pages, search queries, impressions, clicks, and the dedicated generative AI performance report in Search Console where available. Google notes indexing and appearance are not guaranteed. [Google 2026 AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
- **Bing/Copilot:** Use Bing Webmaster Tools Search Performance and AI Performance. AI Performance reports cited pages and grounding query groups across supported Microsoft/partner experiences. Bing says citations are neither clicks nor a ranking or authority measure, and changes do not prove causation. [Bing AI Performance](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/ai-performance-9f8e7d6c)
- **ChatGPT:** Track referral traffic with `utm_source=chatgpt.com` as described by OpenAI. Treat missing referrals as an incomplete view of assistant visibility, not proof of exclusion. [OpenAI publisher FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)
- **Time horizon:** Changes can take hours to months to appear in Google; Google suggests waiting at least a few weeks to assess effects. Measure over time by page and query intent, with conversions alongside visibility. [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

**Decision rule:** prioritize verified crawl/index issues and useful destination pages first. Treat `llms.txt`, AI-only rewrites, mass keyword variants, paid citation promises, and unverified ranking claims as low-value or unsupported until a platform supplies evidence. [Google 2026 AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) · [Bing Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a)
