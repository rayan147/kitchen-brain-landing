Frequently asked questions

The practical questions behind letting a shelf quantity change what you buy.

How is on-hand inventory calculated?

Count an ingredient and record the date. Later purchases add stock; logged waste and completed packing subtract it. The history shows each change.

What do Fresh, Stale, and Never counted mean?

Fresh means the count can be used to reduce buying. Stale means count it again. Never counted means enter a first physical count before using stock in the calculation.

Will a stale count reduce what the shopping list buys?

No. Only trusted on-hand quantity reduces estimated buying. A stale or unsupported estimate can remain visible for context, but it is not allowed to quietly subtract from the buy.

Does a par level automatically add food to the shopping list?

Not today. A par level can mark an ingredient below par, at or above par, unevaluable, or without a par. Buying that tops stock back up to the par level you set is Coming soon. Today the shopping list covers the jobs on the books minus trusted on-hand quantity; it does not yet replenish to par. No date is promised.

What happens when a count is corrected later?

A correction adds a dated record. Earlier counts and the unit costs saved with them stay in the history.

Can I see what the shelf was worth on an earlier date?

Yes. Inventory can show shelf value as of a selected date, using the count and movement history available for that point in time.
