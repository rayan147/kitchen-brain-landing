Frequently asked questions

The practical questions that come up before you bring a real recipe into the room.

What is recipe management software?

Recipe management software keeps ingredients, method, yield, sub-recipes, scaling, and the kitchen-ready version together. In CostCook, that means the recipe the kitchen reads is also the record used for costing.

How does recipe costing work in CostCook?

CostCook follows the purchase price through pack size, usable yield, recipe quantity, portion, and guest count. You can open the arithmetic behind a plate cost or food-cost percentage instead of accepting an unexplained total.

Can I scale a recipe without changing the original?

Yes. Scale by a target amount or by what you have on hand, then use the recalculated quantities as a preview. The saved recipe stays unchanged.

What happens when a yield or ingredient price is missing?

Trim loss and cooking loss remain separate facts. If a line cannot be costed, CostCook names the missing price and labels the total as partial instead of quietly treating the ingredient as free.

Can I reuse sub-recipes and hand recipes to the kitchen?

Yes. A sauce or other sub-recipe can feed several dishes without copying its costing facts into each one. Recipes can also be exported to CSV or printed as clean kitchen sheets stamped with the print time.

Can I see an earlier published recipe?

Yes. Recipe history keeps earlier published versions available to read. The current published version remains clear, so opening history does not silently replace what the kitchen uses.

Will a later price change rewrite a confirmed quote?

No. Confirming an order freezes its plan, math, and money together. CostCook can compare that frozen quote with current prices later without silently rewriting what you originally quoted.
