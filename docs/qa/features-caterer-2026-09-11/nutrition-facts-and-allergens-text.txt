Frequently asked questions

The source, missing-value, printing, and food-safety questions worth settling first.

What is nutrition facts software?

It calculates the nutrients in a recipe from its ingredient quantities and source profiles. CostCook shows four headline macros and the fifteen values carried by a Nutrition Facts panel, per portion or batch, on the recipe you already costed.

Where does CostCook get nutrient values?

You match each ingredient to a USDA FoodData Central profile, including the raw, cooked, or prepared state that fits. That source stays attached to the ingredient and rolls through sub-recipes into the dish.

What happens when a nutrient value is missing?

CostCook leaves the value blank and names the missing profile or conversion. It reports a partial or incomplete result instead of counting the missing value as zero or presenting an incomplete dish as complete.

How does CostCook track allergens?

Fourteen allergen tags live on ingredients and roll up through sub-recipes into recipes and pack lists. The evidence comes from a supplier record, a package label, or a kitchen review. A chef override requires a written reason.

Does CostCook make an allergen-free or compliance claim?

No. Unreviewed ingredients remain visible, and the recipe does not claim to be allergen-free. The printed nutrition sheet also says it is a calculated estimate, not a retail-label regulatory compliance claim.

Can I print a nutrition label today?

Yes. Print nutrition label opens a browser-printable sheet from the recipe, ready for label stock. A direct label-printer integration is still in development and carries no release date.
